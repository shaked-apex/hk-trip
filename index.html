The route · Day one

Tuesday Aug 4 Island Line

The Peak in the morning while the queue is short. A museum in the hot hours. The ferry at sunset.

  1. 10:00

    HKU → Central

    Exit A2 · Island Line towards Chai Wan · 3 stops (Sai Ying Pun, Sheung Wan, Central).

    Directions 10 min · $5.9
  2. 10:30

    Victoria Peak

    What it is: the mountain above the city, and the classic Hong Kong view — skyscrapers, the harbour and the islands all at once. You get up it on a 130-year-old funicular railway that climbs so steeply the buildings outside look tilted. Sky Terrace is the rooftop viewing platform at the top.

    From Central take exit J2, turn right through Chater Garden, then walk up Garden Road — 12 minutes. If it feels too hot, take a taxi, about $35.

    With the online ticket, go straight to the boarding gate and scan from your phone. Sit on the right side going up.

    At the top: Sky Terrace, then Lugard Road — a flat, shaded path. Walk 20 minutes to the first viewpoint and come back.

    Going down: take bus 15 from the stop next to Peak Tower. It has air conditioning and a great view.

    Map Next leg ~$150
  3. 12:30

    Lunch

    What it is: dim sum — small steamed and fried dishes, the local lunch. Two options below: one traditional Cantonese teahouse, one kosher restaurant.

    Lin Heung — dim sum pushed around on trolleys. Just point at what you want. Cash only, about $100. The famous Wellington Street shop has closed and moved to Des Voeux Road Central in Sheung Wan — check it is open before walking over.

    Shalom Grill if you want kosher — 2/F Fortune House, 61 Connaught Rd Central. Phone first; I could not confirm its hours.

    If neither works out, any place with a queue of locals will do. Dim sum is everywhere in this area.

    Lin Heung Shalom Grill
  4. 14:00

    Tai Kwun

    What it is: the former central police station, courthouse and prison of the British era, restored into a free art and culture centre. You walk through the old cell blocks and courtyards, with contemporary art galleries and cafés inside. 10 Hollywood Road, fully air conditioned.

    If you run out of energy, sit in a gallery for an hour. This stop is here for exactly that.

    Map Free
  5. 16:00

    Man Mo Temple

    What it is: a small, dark, working Chinese temple from 1847, thick with incense smoke, dedicated to the gods of literature and of war. Locals still come to pray. Free, and takes 15 minutes to see. Same street, 8 minutes west. Open until 18:00, so 16:00 is comfortable.

    Directions 8 min walk
  6. 16:45

    Star Ferry

    What it is: the old green-and-white passenger ferry that has crossed the harbour since 1888. It is ordinary public transport that happens to be the best view in the city — you cross from the island to the mainland with the skyline on both sides. TST is Tsim Sha Tsui, the waterfront district on the far side.

    Pier 7 to TST. Tap your Octopus, go to the upper deck and sit outside. Eight minutes, and the best light of the day.

    To Pier 7 $5
  7. 17:30

    Back to HKU

    TST → Tsuen Wan Line two stops to Central (via Admiralty) → Island Line towards Kennedy Town → HKU.

    Directions 30 min

The route · Day two

Wednesday Aug 5 Tung Chung Line

Lantau Island. Long rides, but all of them are air conditioned. Before you leave, check np360.com.hk to make sure the cable car is running.

  1. 10:00

    HKU → Tung Chung

    Island Line to Central → follow the covered walkway to Hong Kong Station → Tung Chung Line to the last stop.

    Directions 45 min · $25
  2. 11:00

    Ngong Ping 360 cable car

    What it is: a cable car that carries you over the sea and then over green mountains for 25 minutes, from the town of Tung Chung up to the plateau where the Big Buddha sits. It is the journey itself that is the attraction. Crystal Cabin is the version with a glass floor.

    Exit B, walk left around Citygate, then take the escalators up to the terminal. Five minutes.

    The ride is 25 minutes over the sea and the hills.

    On weekdays it runs until 18:00. Come back in the evening by bus 11.

    A No. 3 typhoon signal closes the cable car even though the rest of the city keeps running. If T3 is up when you wake, switch to the museums below.

    Map $365
  3. 12:00

    Big Buddha

    What it is: a very large bronze Buddha sitting on a hilltop, reached by a staircase of 268 steps. Next to it is Po Lin, a working Buddhist monastery where monks still live — which is why the restaurant there is vegetarian.

    Ten minutes through the village. You can see the statue from everywhere, so just walk towards it.

    268 steps in full sun. Go up slowly with breaks — or stay at the bottom and enjoy it from there. It looks just as impressive from below.

    Lunch at the monastery restaurant — vegetarian, air conditioned, serving 11:30–16:30. Buy a meal ticket at the entrance; it usually also gets you into the hall inside the statue. Around $130.

    Map
  4. 14:20

    Tai O

    What it is: an old fishing village on the far coast, where the houses stand on wooden stilts above the water and are connected by narrow walkways. It is the opposite of the skyscraper city — the Hong Kong that existed before the towers.

    Bus 21 from the terminal next to the cable car — it runs about once an hour, so aim for the 14:20 (or 15:20). Eighteen minutes, ride to the last stop. Tap your Octopus when you get on. $7.9.

    A fishing village built on stilts over the water. A boat tour costs $30 from the stalls on the main street — 20 minutes, the coolest part of the village, and sometimes you see pink dolphins.

    Directions Cash for the boat
  5. 16:15

    Back to HKU

    Bus 11 goes straight to Tung Chung, about 52 minutes, $14. It runs every 10–20 minutes until after midnight, so there is no last bus to worry about — stay as long as you like. Then take the MTR back.

    Directions ~95 min

Getting ready · Step 1

Before you fly

  1. Book the Peak Tram and Sky Terracethepeak.com.hk · about $148 on a weekday, and you enter by scanning the QR code from your phone at the gate. No booking fee. Prices vary by date
  2. Book Ngong Ping 360np360.com.hk · Crystal Cabin round trip $365 (₪143) — the glass-floor one. Standard is about $270 (₪106) for the same route and view. This is the most expensive thing in the plan either way; booking online is cheaper than the counter
  3. Screenshot both ticketsthey still work when the signal is weak
  4. Install MyObservatorytyphoon, rain and heat warnings. Open it every morning
  5. Download the Google offline mapselect all of Hong Kong including Lantau — the signal there is weak
  6. Make two lists in Google MapsSave → New list · "Day 1" and "Day 2". On the day, navigate one stop at a time — that is how public transport works
  7. Buy a cooling towel and electrolytesthe most useful things against the heat, and cheaper at home
  8. Pack a UK plug adapter and a light sweaterthree pins · the air conditioning there is strong

Getting ready · Step 2

At the airport

Do this when you land, before you leave the terminal. Ten minutes in total.

  1. Turn on the eSIMwhile still in the arrivals hall, so you walk out with working navigation
  2. Check your Octopus card has credityou already have the card. Tap it at any Add Value machine to see the balance, and top up with cash if it is under $150 — two days of travel and buses costs about $110
  3. Fill your water bottlethere are water fountains in the terminal
  4. Travel to HKUAirport Express to Hong Kong Station, then Island Line towards Kennedy Town to HKU. Pay with the Octopus

Backup

If plans break

Heavy rain

Showers here are short. Step into an MTR station or a 7-Eleven and wait 20 minutes.

Too hot

Swap the day for two big museums in West Kowloon, a few minutes' walk apart, both fully air conditioned. M+ is modern art and design in a huge new building on the waterfront; the Hong Kong Palace Museum shows imperial treasures on loan from Beijing's Forbidden City. M+ is $190 (₪74) and closed Mondays; the Palace Museum is closed on Tuesdays, so it only works as a Day 2 swap. Book online.

T8 warning

Typhoon or black rain — the city closes. Stay at HKU. At T3 the cable car and the ferries stop, but the MTR, the Peak Tram and everything indoors keep running. The Peak Tram also runs in amber and red rain; only Sky Terrace shuts in black rain.

Feeling ill

Dizziness, nausea, or you stop sweating — get into air conditioning, drink, take electrolytes. If it lasts more than 20 minutes, ask anyone nearby to call 999.

Good to know

  • An Israeli passport enters without a visa, for 90 days.
  • English is enough everywhere in this plan. Signs are in two languages.
  • Everything is done online or by scanning a code — bookings, entry and transport.
  • Very safe for a woman travelling alone, including in the evening.
  • With locals, easy topics work best — food, places, the weather.
  • Two days cost about $870 HKD ≈ 340 shekels, tickets and food included. Carry around $1,100 (≈ 430 shekels) for taxis, drinks and buffer.
  • Quick conversion: $100 HKD ≈ 39 shekels, or 1 shekel ≈ $2.6. Day 1 runs about $274 (₪107), Day 2 about $596 (₪233).
