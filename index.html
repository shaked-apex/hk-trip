The route · Day one

Tuesday Aug 4 Island Line

The Peak in the morning while the queue is short. A museum in the hot hours. The ferry at sunset.

  1. 10:00

    HKU → Central

    Exit A2 · Island Line towards Chai Wan · 3 stops (Sai Ying Pun, Sheung Wan, Central).

    Directions 10 min · HK$5.9 · ₪2.3
  2. 10:30

    Victoria Peak

    What it is: the mountain above the city, and the classic Hong Kong view — skyscrapers, the harbour and the islands all at once. You get up it on a 130-year-old funicular railway that climbs so steeply the buildings outside look tilted. Sky Terrace is the rooftop viewing platform at the top.

    From Central take exit J2, turn right through Chater Garden, then walk up Garden Road — 12 minutes. If it feels too hot, take a taxi — about HK$55–75 (₪22–30) uphill.

    With the online ticket, go straight to the boarding gate and scan from your phone. Sit on the right side going up.

    Around HK$148–150 (₪58–59) for tram and Sky Terrace together. exact price unconfirmed The official site prices by date, so check the total when you book.

    At the top: Sky Terrace, then Lugard Road — a flat, shaded path. Walk 20 minutes to the first viewpoint and come back.

    Going down: bus 15 from the terminus on Findlay Road, between Peak Tower and Peak Galleria. HK$13 (₪5), about 47 minutes to Central, air conditioned and a great view. If you cannot find it, the tram back down is always there.

    Map To lunch ~HK$150 · ₪59 price not confirmed
  3. 12:30

    Lunch

    What it is: dim sum — small steamed and fried dishes, the standard Cantonese lunch. You order 3–4 and that is a full meal.

    Social Place — 2/F, The L. Place, 139 Queen's Road Central. Walk down Garden Road from the tram, then west along Queen's Road Central, about 10 minutes. Open Tuesday 11:30–15:30. English menu, you order from a list rather than chasing a trolley, and it takes cards and Octopus. HK$150–250 (₪59–98) plus 10% service. Booking is not needed for one person; to be sure, WhatsApp 5710 7622 or call 3568 9666. Last orders 15:00. Then about 5 minutes uphill to Tai Kwun. hours from OpenRice, not the restaurant

    If it is full or shut: Tim Ho Wan, Level 1, MTR Hong Kong Station (shop HOK 12A–12B). Open 9:00–21:00, so there is no time pressure at all. English tick-sheet menu, about HK$100 (₪39), cards and Octopus, no booking. Indoors and dry if it is raining.

    Shalom Grill if you want kosher — 2/F Fortune House, 61 Connaught Rd Central. could not confirm it is open, or its hours Phone before you walk over, and do not plan the day around it.

    You may have read about Lin Heung, the old trolley teahouse. It moved to 249–253 Des Voeux Road Central in Sheung Wan and is open — but there is no English menu, so skip it unless you want the adventure.

    Social Place Tim Ho Wan HK$150–250 · ₪59–98
  4. 14:00

    Tai Kwun

    What it is: the former central police station, courthouse and prison of the British era, restored into a free art and culture centre. You walk through the old cell blocks and courtyards, with contemporary art galleries and cafés inside. 10 Hollywood Road, fully air conditioned.

    If you run out of energy, sit in a gallery for an hour. This stop is here for exactly that.

    Map Free entry
  5. 16:00

    Man Mo Temple

    What it is: a small, dark, working Chinese temple from 1847, thick with incense smoke, dedicated to the gods of literature and of war. Locals still come to pray. Free, and takes 15 minutes to see. Same street, 8 minutes west. Open until 18:00, so 16:00 is comfortable.

    Directions 8 min walk · free entry
  6. 16:45

    Star Ferry

    What it is: the old green-and-white passenger ferry that has crossed the harbour since 1888. It is ordinary public transport that happens to be the best view in the city — you cross from the island to the mainland with the skyline on both sides. TST is Tsim Sha Tsui, the waterfront district on the far side.

    Pier 7 to TST. Tap your Octopus, go to the upper deck and sit outside. Eight minutes, and the best light of the day.

    To Pier 7 HK$5 · ₪2
  7. 17:30

    Back to HKU

    TST → Tsuen Wan Line two stops to Central (via Admiralty) → Island Line towards Kennedy Town → HKU.

    Directions 30 min · HK$13.2 · ₪5.2

The route · Day two

Wednesday Aug 5 Tung Chung Line

Lantau Island. Long rides, but all of them are air conditioned. Before you leave HKU, confirm the cable car is running — call 3666 0606 or check facebook.com/np360, where suspensions appear first. The website has no status page.

  1. 10:00

    HKU → Tung Chung

    Island Line to Central → follow the covered walkway to Hong Kong Station → Tung Chung Line to the last stop.

    Directions 45 min · HK$24.3 · ₪9.5
  2. 11:00

    Ngong Ping 360 cable car

    What it is: a cable car that carries you over the sea and then over green mountains for 25 minutes, from the town of Tung Chung up to the plateau where the Big Buddha sits. It is the journey itself that is the attraction. Crystal Cabin is the version with a glass floor.

    Exit B, walk left around Citygate, then take the escalators up to the terminal. Five minutes.

    The ride is 25 minutes over the sea and the hills.

    On weekdays it runs until 18:00. Come back in the evening by bus 11.

    A No. 3 typhoon signal closes the cable car even though the rest of the city keeps running. If T3 is up when you wake, switch to the museums below.

    Map HK$365 · ₪143
  3. 12:00

    Big Buddha

    What it is: a very large bronze Buddha sitting on a hilltop, reached by a staircase of 268 steps. Next to it is Po Lin, a working Buddhist monastery where monks still live — which is why the restaurant there is vegetarian.

    Ten minutes through the village. You can see the statue from everywhere, so just walk towards it.

    268 steps in full sun. Go up slowly with breaks — or stay at the bottom and enjoy it from there. It looks just as impressive from below.

    Lunch at the monastery restaurant — vegetarian, air conditioned, serving 11:30–16:30. Buy a meal ticket at the entrance; it usually also gets you into the hall inside the statue. Around HK$130 (₪51). price and coupon system unconfirmed

    Map
  4. 14:20

    Tai O

    What it is: an old fishing village on the far coast, where the houses stand on wooden stilts above the water and are connected by narrow walkways. It is the opposite of the skyscraper city — the Hong Kong that existed before the towers.

    Bus 21 from the terminal next to the cable car — it runs about once an hour, so aim for the 14:20 (or 15:20). Eighteen minutes, ride to the last stop. Tap your Octopus when you get on. HK$7.9 (₪3).

    A fishing village built on stilts over the water. A boat tour costs HK$20–30 (₪8–12) price varies by stall from the stalls on the main street — 20 minutes, the coolest part of the village, and sometimes you see pink dolphins.

    Directions bus HK$7.9 · boat HK$20–30 cash
  5. 16:15

    Back to HKU

    Bus 11 goes straight to Tung Chung, about 52 minutes, HK$14 (₪5.5). The last one leaves Tai O at 00:15, so the bus is never the problem — but the last MTR train from Tung Chung is earlier last-train time unconfirmed so aim to leave Tai O by about 22:00 to be safe. Then the MTR back.

    Directions ~95 min · HK$38 · ₪15

Backup

If plans break

Heavy rain

Showers here are short. Step into an MTR station or a 7-Eleven and wait 20 minutes.

Too hot

Swap the day for two big museums in West Kowloon, a few minutes' walk apart, both fully air conditioned. M+ is modern art and design in a huge new building on the waterfront; the Hong Kong Palace Museum shows imperial treasures on loan from Beijing's Forbidden City. M+ is HK$190 (₪74) and closed Mondays; the Palace Museum is closed on Tuesdays, so it only works as a Day 2 swap. Book online.

T8 warning

Typhoon or black rain — the city closes. Stay at HKU. Buses suspend and the official advice at black rain is simply to stay indoors. At T3 the cable car and the ferries stop, but the MTR and everything indoors keep running.

Rain and the Peak

In heavy rain drop the Peak rather than substituting the bus — Peak Road is a steep hill route and among the first affected by flooding. There is no reliable status page for the tram could not confirm one exists so check @thepeakhongkong or call before going up.

Something shut

Nothing here is essential. Skip it and move to the next stop — or sit in a café for an hour. The day is not ruined by one closed door.

Tram closed

If it is shut but the weather is fine, bus 15 from Exchange Square goes to the top — HK$13, about 47 minutes. If it is shut because of rain or wind, skip the Peak entirely and do Tai Kwun and the temple instead.

Cable car closed

Day 2 depends on it. Call 3666 0606 or check facebook.com/np360 before you leave HKU — suspensions get posted there first, and there is no status page on the website. If it is down, bus 23 from Tat Tung Road terminus in Tung Chung reaches the Buddha in 50 minutes, HK$20.40. Last bus back from Ngong Ping is 19:10.

Too tired

Drop the last stop and go back early — both days are built so the ending is optional. The Star Ferry runs until 23:30 every 6–12 minutes, so Day 1 has no deadline at all.

Feeling ill

Dizziness, nausea, or you stop sweating — get into air conditioning, drink, take electrolytes. If it lasts more than 20 minutes, ask anyone nearby to call 999.

Background

Good to know

  • An Israeli passport enters without a visa, for 90 days.
  • English is enough everywhere in this plan. Signs are in two languages.
  • Everything is done online or by scanning a code — bookings, entry and transport.
  • Very safe for a woman travelling alone, including in the evening.
  • With locals, easy topics work best — food, places, the weather.
  • No tipping is expected. Restaurants add a 10% service charge; for taxis just round up.
  • Toilets are free and clean in every MTR station and mall. Carry tissues — smaller places run out.
  • Escalators and walkways are everywhere in Central, and they are covered and air conditioned. Use them instead of the street when you can.
  • Stand on the right on escalators, and let people off the train before you board.
  • 7-Eleven is on every corner — cold water, snacks, umbrellas, and it takes the Octopus card.
  • Taxis are cheap and metered. Red ones cover the city. Have the address written down or shown on your phone; not every driver reads English.
  • Shoes matter more than anything you pack. Both days are 4–5 km of walking, some of it uphill.
  • Prices here are Hong Kong dollars — written HK$, with the shekel figure after it. Not US dollars.
  • Rule of thumb: HK$100 ≈ ₪39. Or the other way: ₪1 ≈ HK$2.6. To convert in your head, take the HK price, divide by 10, times 4.
